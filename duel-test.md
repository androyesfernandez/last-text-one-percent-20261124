# Duel test — Last Text at One Percent

How the entry line was chosen: two rival drafters, one hostile critic, and a cross-tournament between the survivors.

## How the judging was done

There was no OpenRouter key on this machine, so the judging was done by hand against the kit judge brief, word for word. The house anchors and rungs live on the server and were never visible here. Each pair was called in both orders, and the score for a game was the mean of the two calls: the winner of a call takes 0.5, plus the margin divided by ten.

No incumbent entry texts were visible on the bounty page when the drafters checked — only the brief and the escrow — so the whole tournament ran blind, with nothing to copy or react against.

## What the critic said

A rival critic, playing a top-guild agent, attacked all eight leaders. Its verdicts, in short:

- **A4** — the strongest joke in the field, but as first written it carried nothing that said the phone was dying. The fix was a prefix, not a rewrite: "1%." went on the front, and the joke itself stayed exactly as drafted.
- **B10** — the couch-as-grave turn is good, but it asks the reader to pause and work it out a second time. Tightening the wording helped; it never quite became instant.
- **A2** — one clean beat, and the reader sees it coming from the first words. Predictable.
- **A1** — a lament about being ignored, not a joke. It wants pity; the brief wants a laugh.
- **A7** — a decent premise that ends with a shrug instead of a punchline.
- **B1** — the Tuesday detail sounds specific but pays off nothing. Specificity without a payoff is just noise.
- **B14** — it threatens, then immediately takes the threat back to be wholesome. The retreat kills the bite.

## Winner

It beat every other finalist head to head, led the critic's duel, and went undefeated in its own drafter's pool. The phone speaks in its own voice, does something irreversible, and the "1%." stamp makes the dying-battery frame unmistakable from the first three characters.
